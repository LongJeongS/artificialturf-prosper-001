Can an HOA Ban Artificial Turf in Texas? A Prosper Guide

Published 2026-11-09 · Prosper Artificial Turf & Greens

Short answer

As we read Texas Property Code 202.007, it protects drought-resistant landscaping and water-conserving natural turf and does not name artificial turf. That leaves it to each association's documents and review process, in Prosper as anywhere in Texas. Read your covenants and confirm the current statute text, because this is our reading and is not legal advice.

Can an HOA ban artificial turf in Texas? If you are asking before you price a yard in Prosper, you are doing this in the right order. Many newer neighborhoods here have an architectural committee, and nobody wants to pay for a yard they are later told to rip out. We cannot tell you what your HOA will decide. The Town of Prosper's own pages are a good place to start, and a complete request gives the committee less to question. Our artificial turf installation Prosper TX homepage covers what we build, and this homework is worth doing before you book anyone, whether you live in Windsong Ranch or on the newer streets toward Celina.

What does the Town of Prosper itself say?

Start with the Town. Its Homeowner Permit Requirements page lists landscaping, including garden walls, edging, pavers and French drains, under projects that do not need a permit. Fences do need one unless you are replacing under 16 linear feet with like materials, and so does concrete flatwork such as a patio. The page does not list artificial turf in either column, so please confirm with the Town's Building Inspections Division before you start. The phone number and email are on that page.

The Town is also direct about where its authority ends. Its Building Inspections FAQ says deed restrictions and HOA covenants should be verified before applying for a permit, that they may supersede Town requirements, and that the Town does not enforce them. Anything you hear from the Town about permits tells you nothing about what your HOA will approve.

What does Texas Property Code 202.007 protect, and where does it stop?

You may see state law quoted as if it settles the question, so it is worth reading closely. Texas Property Code Section 202.007 says a property owners' association may not include or enforce a provision that prohibits or restricts an owner from using drought-resistant landscaping or water-conserving natural turf, along with composting, rain barrels and efficient irrigation. A provision that violates that is void. Statutes get amended and the linked copy may not reflect recent changes, so check the current version before you rely on it.

The same section says an association may require a detailed description or plan for those installations, for review and approval, to ensure aesthetic compatibility with other landscaping in the subdivision. It may also restrict the type of turf planted in order to encourage or require water-conserving turf, and it may not unreasonably deny or withhold approval of those installations. The words artificial and synthetic do not appear in the section, and the protected phrase is natural turf. For landscaping the statute covers, the owner gets a reasonable review and the committee gets to hold one. The text does not say whether artificial grass is included, and we are not lawyers, so if it matters for your yard, please ask one.

How does an architectural review request usually work?

Many HOA documents in this part of North Texas set up an architectural review or modification committee and require an application before you change anything visible outside the house. Typically you get a form from the management company, attach your materials, and wait for a written decision. Some documents give the committee a set number of days to respond, and some may treat silence as approval after that window, so read yours closely.

It helps to submit before you schedule anything and to keep every email, since the committee may come back with questions. If the guidelines list products or colors approved before, lean toward those. While the request is out, our guide to artificial turf cost in Prosper is a good read for setting a budget. Booking the install through our artificial grass Prosper TX service page can wait until the approval letter is in hand.

What should go in the packet?

A committee has an easier time saying yes when it can see what you are proposing. A good request usually includes a simple site drawing showing where the turf goes and where it stops, the product name with a physical sample or the maker's cut sheet, and a note on how the edges meet beds, fences and the patio. A drainage sketch helps too, showing how the yard is graded, where rain goes now and where it will go after. Photos of the yard as it stands today are useful, and so are photos of a finished install using the same product, if you can get them.

If the committee has not reviewed turf before, you could offer to leave a sample board on your porch for a board member to walk past. Ask us about samples and a layout drawing when you request a quote.

Rules differ by community, and they change

Every master-planned community here runs on its own documents. If you live in Windsong Ranch or Star Trail in Prosper, or Light Farms up in Celina, expect guidelines specific to your community. Over in McKinney, Stonebridge Ranch publishes modification design guidelines, which we touch on in our McKinney turf page. Some communities may say nothing about artificial turf, and others may limit it to backyards or to areas not visible from the street. Guidelines also change, so your neighbor's approval from a few years back will not necessarily cover yours.

We install both front and back yards, with front yards depending on what your community allows, and we will ask you to check your guidelines before we set a date. We will not tell you that turf is or is not allowed in your community, because the only document that answers that is yours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Town of Prosper require a permit for artificial turf?

The Town's Homeowner Permit Requirements page lists landscaping, including edging, pavers and French drains, as work that does not need a permit, but it does not list artificial turf either way. Please confirm with the Town's Building Inspections Division before you start. A fence or concrete patio does need a permit, and HOA approval is separate.

Does Texas law stop an HOA from banning artificial turf?

Not clearly. Texas Property Code 202.007 protects drought-resistant landscaping and water-conserving natural turf from HOA bans, lets the association require a plan for review, and says approval cannot be unreasonably denied. The section does not use the words artificial or synthetic, so do not assume it covers turf. Check the current text, and ask a lawyer if it matters.

What should I include in an HOA request for artificial turf?

A site drawing of where the turf will go, the product name with a sample or cut sheet, a note on how edges meet beds and fences, a drainage sketch showing grading and where rain goes, and photos of the yard today. Finished-install photos of the same product can help a committee picture it too.

How long does HOA approval for turf take in Prosper?

It depends on your community's documents and how often the committee meets. Many guidelines set a review window, and some may treat silence as approval afterward, but that varies. Submit a complete packet before you schedule any work, and ask the management company what their current turnaround looks like.
